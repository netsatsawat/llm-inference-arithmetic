Can the benchmark prove its own claim?

Convert the percentages back into items before believing anything. The unpaired test assumes the two models were scored on different samples. They were scored on the same ones, so it is the wrong test, and its p-value is a ceiling rather than a verdict. The correct test is McNemar's, which needs to know how often the two models disagreed item by item. Published tables never carry that, so the last control lets you assume a rate and see what it would change.
